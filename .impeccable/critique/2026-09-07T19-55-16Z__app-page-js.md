## Anti-Patterns Verdict

The homepage no longer reads as a generic shader landing page. The field image and revised archive framing give it a specific conservation point of view. It still reads as several impressive components assembled into one scroll rather than one authored world. The deterministic scan returned no findings for the reviewed homepage components.

## Overall Impression

The first two chapters now do real work: a living organism establishes emotional stakes, then field documentation explains the method. The biggest remaining opportunity is to turn every following section into a consequence of that one premise rather than a separate destination card.

## What is Working

- The dawn pollinator hero has a single focal point, usable negative space and a credible image-led mood.
- Field documentation gives the project a human method and makes the archive feel earned.
- The restored botanical border makes the globe read as a specimen/archive object rather than a generic 3D canvas.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The story still fractures after the globe
**Why it matters:** the curve marquee, books, six pastel bento cards and engraving footer introduce competing aesthetics instead of advancing the field-to-archive-to-action narrative.
**Fix:** replace the bento grid with three editorial chapters—Explore the collection, Learn through stories, Protect habitats—each tied to a real image or single visual treatment.

### [P1] The first promise is still too poetic
**Why it matters:** “Blooming Stories” is memorable but does not say what AquaMundi does until the following section.
**Fix:** place one short factual sentence adjacent to the hero CTA: map national flowers, document pollinators, and turn them into public stories and learning tools.

### [P2] Forced loading undercuts the premium opening
**Why it matters:** `app/page.js` deliberately holds the visitor for five seconds even when the visual page is ready.
**Fix:** make the loader dismiss when critical hero assets and translations are ready, keeping only a short transition.

### [P2] Footer motion is source-verified but not visually verified
**Why it matters:** the burn/front effect and footer text now have correct stacking in CSS, but smoothness has not been checked in a rendered browser.
**Fix:** inspect its viewport entry at desktop and mobile, then tune only the visible defect.

### [P3] One visual language needs to win
**Why it matters:** the rich documentary image, pastel bento motifs and ornate mountain engraving each carry different levels of realism.
**Fix:** make the archive’s field-documentary palette and paper/ink material the governing system; retire motif-only backgrounds where they do not explain content.

## Persona Red Flags

### Jordan (first-time visitor)
The hero has strong atmosphere but the primary action is a Pollinator Corridor link, not the archive journey promised by the first headline. Jordan may not know why they should leave AquaMundi before understanding it.

### Casey (distracted mobile visitor)
The full hero plus forced five-second loading and dense navigation delay the first useful action. The image is responsive in source, but the footer reveal and globe require rendered mobile verification.

### Sam (keyboard and screen-reader user)
The new hero and field image have useful alt text and focus styles. The custom globe and curved loop still need explicit keyboard/fallback verification before they can be considered equivalent to standard navigation.

## Minor Observations

- The field section headline is long and should be checked at tablet widths.
- The utility music toggle competes visually with the new documentary opening.
- The hero wordmark and global navigation need a live overlap check at short viewport heights.

## Questions to Consider

- Should the first CTA keep people inside AquaMundi’s archive before sending them to the external corridor?
- Which one deserves to be the visual signature: field-documentary photography or illustrated archive material?
- Can books, game and shop be reframed as outcomes of the archive rather than equal first-order choices?
